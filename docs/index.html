I.The Code

DyonLaw is organized into four books, each a complete and self-contained instrument of governance. Together they constitute the supreme law of the polity.

Book I

Constitution

The foundational charter — the source and limit of all lawful authority within the Departement.

Book II

Civil Code

The law of persons, property, obligations, and the ordinary conduct of civil life.

Book III

Penal Code

Offences against the order, and the punishments the law permits the state to impose.

Book IV

Procedure

The manner in which law is made, enforced, amended, and adjudicated.

II.The Ten Fundamental Laws

Seven axiomatic laws stand unamendable at the foundation of the code — remove one and the system collapses. Three supplementary laws guide its daily operation.

I. CausalityAxiomatic. Every effect has a cause; no artifact enters the Code without a recorded origin.
II. EntropyAxiomatic. Order requires effort; disorder is the default. Cleanup is a civic duty.
III. GravityAxiomatic. Every grant of authority carries an equal obligation; privilege without duty is void.
IV. TimeAxiomatic. Time is the only non-renewable resource; the polity acts early and correctly.
V. IdentityAxiomatic. A polity is defined by what it ships; the unshipped does not exist.
VI. CodeAxiomatic. Working code is law; where documents and code diverge, code prevails.
VII. ChaosAxiomatic. Chaos is order over-applied — judged by outcome, not intent.
VIII. MotionSupplementary. Nothing moves without an initial impulse; momentum is kept by the community.
IX. FocusSupplementary. Attention is the scarcest currency; review goes to the highest-value work first.
X. SilenceSupplementary. Silence is golden — but never itself an admission of guilt.

III.Branches & Instruments of Governance

Authority is exercised only through codified instruments, administered by three branches.

Branch

Legislature

The amendments repository — sole power to enact, amend, and repeal articles by contributor supermajority.

Branch

Executive

The enforcement repository — sets enforcement priorities and signs instruments into law.

Branch

Judiciary

Pull-request review — challenged instruments are adjudicated; merged amendments form binding precedent.

  • Amendment — proposed in writing with rationale; requires supermajority of contributors (Art. 5).
  • Emergency Amendment — fast-tracked procedure during active entropy events.
  • Enforcement Action — filed as an issue in the enforcement repository.
  • Appeal — an enforcement issue tagged appeal (Procedure 403).
  • Judicial Review — every contributor may challenge an instrument by pull request.

On the Nature of This Publication

DyonLaw is a work of the Departement-Of-Dyon and is published for study, reference, and the edification of all who encounter it. It describes a legal order — it does not create binding obligations outside that order. The text is offered in good faith and may be amended as the polity develops.
